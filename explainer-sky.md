# Sample: why the sky is blue

Sample material for a first explainer (say that it is a sample). Every fact below has its source: Wikipedia,
"Diffuse sky radiation", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diffuse_sky_radiation (read 2026-11-18).

**Question:** Why is the sky blue?

**Answer in one change:** the air scatters blue light much more than red, so blue reaches the eye from every part of
the sky, not only from the sun.

**Facts** (each is in the article)

- During daylight the sky is blue because of Rayleigh scattering.
- The air scatters short-wavelength light more efficiently than longer wavelengths.
- Blue light has shorter wavelengths, so it is scattered more strongly than red or green light.
- Looking at the sky away from the sun, a person sees blue.
- The blue sky still holds light of every visible wavelength, strongest around 450–485 nm, the wavelengths of blue.
- Lord Rayleigh explained the blue colour in 1871.

**The object to follow:** one beam of sunlight entering the air.

**A line for the end:** "Blue is the light that got knocked off course."
